# Introduction to ColdFront

## Description

[ColdFront](https://docs.coldfront.dev/en/stable-1.1.x/) is an open source resource and allocation management system designed to provide a central portal for administration, reporting, and measuring scientific impact of cyberinfrastructure resources. ColdFront was created to help high performance computing (HPC) centers manage access to a diverse set of resources across large groups of users and provide a rich set of extensible meta data for comprehensive reporting.

[Link to UC Merced ColdFront](https://coldfront.rc.ucmerced.edu)

## What you may have access to.

Within logging into ColdFront (instructions detailed at a later segment), you may be able to see these things below:

1. Projects
* What projects you and other researchers have access to.
2. Your available compute and storage
* This shows all of the available compute (such as the Pinnacles & MERCED Clusters) and storage (such as BorgStore) you have available for your projects and other assignments.
* Clicking on them shows who has access and descriptions of what they are and their purpose.
* ColdFront is primarily a platform designed to monitor the compute and storage-usage for projects and used to create submit jobs.
3. Common Actions
* If you ever have trouble finding your Projects or your available Compute and Storage, you can always click the options below "Common Actions" such as the "Manage Projects" option to see your active and inactive projects more easily, or the "Your Access" option, which allows you to see your active compute access and storage more easily. You can always click "Home" to return to the home screen.

## More Options

At the upper-left part of the screen, if you click "Project" it will show a dropdown menu consisting of these things:

1. Projects, which was mentioned earlier.
2. Allocations, which is your available compute and storage.
3. Usage
* This option provides you all of the Compute and Storage that you have used for your projects. It shows your core-hours recorded for you over the month as well as average physical storage usage.
4. and Resources
* The resources option provides you with a view of what compute nodes are being actively used as well as inactive nodes. Additionally, it shows what shared storage nodes are being used as well.
* You can pick which cluster context is being shown, what nodes does the Pinnacles and MERCED Clusters have/are being used as well as viewing the specific resources/nodes being used under the "Resource" dropdown menu.

## Logging into ColdFront

1. ColdFront, similar to all UC Merced Campus login systems, uses the SSH login system. You need your UC Merced User ID as well as your password.
2. Additionally, you must be connected to the UC Merced VPN even if on or off campus. Instructions to download and login to the VPN are found [here](docs/hpcdocs/HPC-clusters/campus-clusters.md) underneath "UC Merced VPN Installation and Connection Instructions".
3. After connecting your device to the UC Merced VPN, you may start logging into ColdFront at [this link](coldfront.rc.ucmerced.edu).
4. Sign in with your UC Merced login. It will direct you to "CILogin"
5. When directed to CILogin, you will need to select University of California - Merced to continue.
6. After completing these steps, you have successfully logged into ColdFront.

## Asking to upgrade to Principal Instructor (PI) status

If you are only a member and not a Principal Instructor, you can request Principal Instructor status through the profile menu:

1. On the upper-right of your screen, your account name will have a dropdown menu. Click it to show that menu.
2. Once clicked, there should be an option named "User profile."
3. After clicking on "User profile," your account details are shown and you may request PI status.
